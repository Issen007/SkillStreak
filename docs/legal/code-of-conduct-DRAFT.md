## For players — read this part first

Hey! This app is for training, streaks, and hanging out with your team —
not a place to worry about. Here's the short version of how to be a good
teammate here, and what actually happens if something goes wrong.

### 1. Be kind. That's most of it.

Cheer people on. Celebrate someone else's streak, not just your own. If a
teammate's having a rough week, "nice effort today" goes a long way. Most
of what makes this app fun is other people — treat it that way.

### 2. No bullying, no harassment, no being mean on purpose

That includes:

- Making fun of someone, their training, their screen name, or how they
  look in a clip.
- Excluding someone on purpose or getting others to gang up on them.
- Sending mean, threatening, or scary messages.
- Anything that would count as bullying anywhere else — this app doesn't
  get a pass just because it's about training.

If you wouldn't say it to a teammate's face at practice, don't type it
here either.

### 3. Some words just won't send

If you try to send a message with certain words in it, it won't go
through — you'll get asked to write it a different way. This isn't there
to catch you out; it's just a basic safety net. **Here's the honest bit:
this filter only catches specific words, not every mean thing someone
could say.** So it's not the whole plan — see the next section for the
rest of it.

### 4. Report, and block — what they actually do

If a message or a clip feels wrong, you can **report** it. Here's exactly
what happens, no more and no less:

- Nobody finds out it was you. Not the person you reported, not your
  team.
- The reported clip gets hidden from the whole team right away.
- A reported message doesn't disappear automatically — but your report
  is real, and it does go somewhere (see below).
- An email goes to that player's own parent, and to your coach if the app
  has their email on file. It's the honest way of getting an adult in the
  loop.

**What we won't pretend to promise:** we can't guarantee someone reads
that email quickly, or at all, or that anything happens right away. If
something's serious, don't just report it and wait — **tell your coach or
a parent directly, too.** Reporting in the app is one good step, not the
only one.

You can also **block** someone. That means you stop seeing their
messages — just for you. It's quiet: they're never told you blocked them,
and it doesn't affect anyone else on the team. If someone's messages are
bothering you, blocking them is a completely fine thing to do, any time,
for any reason.

### 5. What happens outside the app stays outside the app — and vice versa

- **Only your own verified team can ever see what you post here** — your
  training, your messages, your clips. Nobody outside your team, and
  nobody outside the app at all, unless someone actively takes it out of
  the app themselves (a screenshot, for example — the app can't prevent
  that, but sharing your teammates' stuff outside the team breaks this
  rule).
- **Don't share what happens in this app somewhere else** — a teammate's
  message, a clip, anything — without asking them first. Your team chat
  and feed are a closed space on purpose; help keep it that way.
- **Don't share other people's personal info** — real names (if you know
  them), phone numbers, addresses, anything like that — even inside the
  team chat. Screen names exist so everyone gets to choose how much they
  share.

### 6. Posting clips

- Only post clips of yourself, or clips where everyone in them is okay
  with being posted.
- Keep it about training — drills, fitness, floorball skills. That's what
  this feed is for.
- You can delete your own clip, any time, for any reason, no explaining
  needed — it's gone immediately.
- Clips don't stay up forever anyway — they're automatically removed
  after a while on their own.

### 7. If something feels really wrong

Report/block are good first steps, and this app's coach and your
parent(s) genuinely do get a heads-up when you report something. But if
something feels urgent, scary, or like a real safety problem — **please
talk to a coach, a parent, or another trusted adult directly, the same
way you would if this happened at practice.** This app is a tool to help
you train and stay connected with your team — it's not the only place to
go when something serious happens, and it's not meant to be.

## For parents and guardians — the same rules, from your side

Everything above is written directly for your child, in plain language,
because we think kids read (and follow) rules better when they're written
to them, not about them. This section is the same material, from your
perspective, plus what you can actually do if something goes wrong.

### What this Code of Conduct actually covers

Team chat and the video clip feed — the two places players can post
freeform content that other teammates see. Training logs, streaks, and
the team point pool aren't covered here since there's no freeform
player-to-player content involved in those.

### What's structurally true, not just promised

- **Team-only, closed by design.** A team is only reachable by its own
  private invite code — there's no public directory, no cross-team
  visibility, and no way for your child's content to be seen by anyone
  outside their own verified team through this app.
- **Screen-name-first identity.** Your child's real name is never shown
  to teammates unless you explicitly choose to include it and even then
  it's visible only to their coach, in an admin-only view.
- **A word filter on chat**, and **automatic metadata stripping on video
  clips** (so a clip recorded at home can't leak your home's location,
  even though the app never asks for or stores location data at all).

### What moderation here honestly does and doesn't do

We'd rather be upfront about this than let it come as a surprise later:

- A report (from any player, about any message or clip) triggers a
  **best-effort email** to your address on file, and to the team's coach
  if the app has one recorded for them.
- A reported **video clip is auto-hidden** from the team immediately. A
  reported **chat message is not** auto-hidden (a deliberate choice — see
  `docs/adr/0007-team-chat.md` if you want the full reasoning; in short,
  we didn't want to give any one child the power to instantly silence a
  teammate).
- **There is currently no dedicated moderation team reviewing reports on
  a guaranteed timeline.** The email notification is real and is sent —
  whether and how quickly it's acted on depends on whoever receives it
  (you, or the team's coach). There is also no in-app appeal process yet
  if a hide turns out to have been a mistake.
- The chat filter catches specific flagged words, not every way a child
  could be unkind in "clean" language. It's a real, useful layer, not a
  complete one.

### What you can do

- **Block, on your child's behalf or with them** — a personal, silent,
  immediate mute of one teammate's messages, from your child's own view
  only. No report needed, no reason required.
- **Report** anything that seems wrong — you (or your child) will get a
  best-effort email confirming a report went in, and it's genuinely
  reviewed by whoever reads it, but see the honest limits above.
  Reporting is anonymous to the reported player.
- **Talk to the team's coach directly** — for most youth teams, the coach
  is the adult with real, immediate context on what's actually happening
  among the kids, and can act faster than any in-app mechanism.
- **Ask us to remove a specific video clip** — the fastest path is having
  your child delete their own clip in-app (immediate, no waiting), but
  you can also reach out via the contact method in the Terms of Service
  if that's not possible.
- **Ask us to delete your child's entire account** — a real, working
  self-service flow exists for this (30-day confirm-and-grace-period,
  fully explained in `docs/legal/terms-of-service-DRAFT.md`, Section 7).
- **For anything urgent or safety-related** — a real threat, grooming
  behavior, anything you'd otherwise report to a school or the police —
  **please don't rely on this app's in-app report/block tools as your
  first or only response.** They're a genuine, useful signal, not
  designed or resourced to be an emergency response system. Go directly
  to the coach, the relevant authorities, or both, the same as you would
  outside this app.

### One more honest note

This app is currently built and run by a small team — not a company with
a dedicated trust-and-safety department. That's not a reason to be more
worried than the specific limitations above already describe, but it is
why this document says "best-effort" instead of promising response times
it can't back up. We'd rather tell you that plainly than have you
discover it the hard way.
